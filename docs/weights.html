The seven-node product (1+1)*(1+1) has operation counts (2 additions, 1 multiplication, 4 leaves). Its addition-only equivalents have (3, 0, 4). They tie exactly when addition and multiplication have the same cost. No rule path connects them inside seven nodes.

In this box, 224 weight triples have a positive gap and 288 do not. The two semantic groups each win at 288 triples because the 64 tie triples count for both groups. Wins are memberships, not disjoint probabilities.

A dominated count vector cannot win under any strictly positive weights. A vector with zero wins in this finite box need not be dominated, nor can it be ruled out at every positive weight.

Exactly 512 integral (+, *, leaf) triples in {1,...,8} cubed. The fixed node budget is 3, 5 or 7. Complete ordered arithmetic trees over 0, 1, x, y, + and *, with the same bidirectional base rewrites and full reverse-zero introduction. Semantic identities are over rational arithmetic, not IEEE floating point.

Dominance means another distinct count vector is no larger in any coordinate and smaller in at least one. With strictly positive weights this vector is always strictly cheaper. We do not equate nondominance with winning in the box, nor an absence of wins with global impossibility. Win counts count weight triples, not expression counts or probabilities. Costs and selected optima can change without changing the graph or shortest distances.

All score arithmetic is exact at these small bounds. The explicit grammar grows exponentially under a generalized budget and remains capped at seven nodes. This is not a new algorithm, learned optimizer, e-graph, tensor engine or compiler benchmark. See WEIGHTS.md, CONTRACT.md and SOURCES.md.
